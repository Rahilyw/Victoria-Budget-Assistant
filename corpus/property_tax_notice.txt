2026 CITY OF VICTORIA PROPERTY TAXES
Message from the Mayor
Dear Victorians,
I’m pleased to share highlights of Budget 2026, the final annual 
budget from this Mayor and Council.
Despite ongoing global economic pressures and rising costs, we have 
worked closely with staff to find savings and make our services more 
lean and efficient, to limit the 2026 property tax increase to 7.28 per 
cent – 5.06% for police services and 2.22% for all other City services 
– on average 9.34% for residents and 4.78% for businesses. 
Our goal is to deliver high-quality programs and services you  
can rely on every day while keeping Victoria safe, connected and  
livable. We are also planning for the future, which means investing  
in maintaining aging infrastructure and creating new facilities  
to meet community needs.
In 2026, Council will invest $394.1 million on operating expenses  
to deliver over 200 services and programs and $160.1 million on 
capital improvements. 
We know that housing remains a priority for Victorians, and our 
investments in 2025 have approved 2,509 homes for Victorians.  
This year, we are investing $3.3 million in city-wide and area 
planning, and a variety of housing and tenant supports.  
Community safety and wellbeing remains a key concern, so we 
continue to invest in a range of programs and services that make  
our neighbourhoods safer and more livable. For example: 
• $4.3 million in community-driven initiatives and economic activity 
with the City’s grant programs that include Direct Award Grants, 
Festival Project Grants, Community Garden Volunteer Grants and 
My Great Neighbourhood Grants.
• $785,000 for the City’s OUR DWTN revitalization program  
to invest in the downtown core with a focus on beautification, 
programming and family-friendly activities such as Summer  
at Ship Point. 
• $500,000 for streetlight replacement and increased public space 
cleaning and enhancements. 
• $2.7 million to upgrade and make public washrooms more 
accessible.
• $1.1 million to expand the City’s pedestrian network of accessible 
sidewalks and pathways to support a welcoming experience for 
walking and rolling, with an additional investment of $836,000 
to create new crosswalks and upgrade crossing locations, and 
another $508,000 to replace and widen existing sidewalks. 
• $450,000 for traffic calming measures; installing speed humps, 
prioritizing installations on streets adjacent to schools, parks
and streets with higher speeds and volumes – this investment 
also supports curb extensions or medians to enhance pedestrian 
crossings and safety. 
• $1.22 million to community partnerships aimed at supporting 
vulnerable residents and enhancing overall wellbeing. 
• $78.05 million to Victoria Police (accounting for 69.5% of the 
total increase in property taxes). 
• $24.8 million to fire services and $9.1 million to community safety 
and bylaw services. 
A sustainable, supported city needs investment in infrastructure, 
climate action, Reconciliation and recreation, so we have dedicated: 
• $44.7 million to rehabilitate Victoria’s underground infrastructure, 
including replacing and upgrading water, stormwater and  
sewer mains.
• $15 million for road restoration and rehabilitation, resulting 
in approximately seven kilometres of road paving, including 
segments of Blanshard Street, Cook Street and Richmond Road. 
• $2.3 million to advance the City’s low-carbon fleet to meet 
the municipality’s greenhouse gas emission reduction targets, 
including installing electric vehicle charging infrastructure and 
building out the EV charging network. 
• $200,000 for the annual Reconciliation Grant for the Songhees 
Nation and the Xwsepsum Nation; the City’s Reconciliation 
Contribution Fund also continues – please see the back page on 
how to contribute to this voluntary Fund. 
• $209.2 million for ongoing design and construction of the  
Crystal Pool Replacement Project.
• $8.6 million to upgrade and improve accessibility to Royal Athletic 
Park’s seating and washrooms. 
• $750,000 for a new off-leash dog park in Harris Green.
Our 2026 budget illustrates ambitious attention to the demands of 
our diverse, growing city. It is an honour to serve as your mayor and 
a privilege to work alongside you as we continue to address social 
challenges and create an intentional, thriving community for all.
Marianne Alto  
Victoria Mayor

In 2026, the increase in revenue needed from property 
taxes is $14.185 million over 2025, resulting in an overall  
tax increase of 7.28%.
Council has distributed this increase at 9.34% to 
residential properties and 4.78% to business properties.
This will result in an average property tax increase  
of $323 for an average residential property assessed  
at $1,015,000 and an average increase of $427 for  
a typical business property assessed at $721,000.
Please note that these are estimates only and each 
property will vary based on a number of factors. If your 
property assessment change (determined through BC 
Assessment) is equal to the average in its property 
classification your property tax change will be close to 
the average. If your property assessment increased or 
decreased at a greater or lower rate than the average, 
your property tax will adjust accordingly.

Reconciliation Contribution Fund
In 2015, the 94 Calls to Action of the Truth and Reconciliation 
Commission of Canada made clear that municipalities like the 
City of Victoria have a key role to play in reconciliation, and 
that reconciliation is about more than words, it must also 
include actions.
What is the Reconciliation Contribution Fund?
In 2022, the City of Victoria established the Reconciliation Contribution 
Fund to create an opportunity for residential and commercial property 
owners to contribute a voluntary amount to the Songhees Nation and 
Xwsepsәm Nation alongside their City of Victoria property taxes. 
Your voluntary contribution will be provided to the Songhees Nation 
and Xwsepsәm Nation, in addition to the City’s annual $200,000 
Reconciliation Grant. Both programs are an effort to start recognizing 
that the wealth generated by our City and its residents – including in the 
form of property ownership and associated taxes – is rooted in the lands 
and waters of the Lək̓ʷəŋən (Lekwungen) peoples.
Why is the Reconciliation Contribution Fund needed?
The City’s Reconciliation Contribution Fund was developed based on 
public input. People asked for a tangible way for Victoria residents and 
businesses to make a meaningful contribution towards reconciliation. 
The Fund will be used by the Songhees Nation and Xwsepsәm Nation  
to achieve the goals and aims of their communities.
How does the program work? 
Your voluntary contribution is to be made in addition to, and separately 
from, your annual property tax payment. The full value of your 
voluntary contribution will be provided directly to the Xwsepsәm 
Nation and Songhees Nation.
Some Victoria property owners have opted to contribute an amount 
equal to five or 10 per cent of their property taxes. You may contribute 
any amount – every dollar is welcome.
